# CONSTITUTION.md — Revenue OS Operating Constitution

**Source:** Synthesized and restructured from the Revenue OS handoff (Sections 0, 3, 6). This is
the stable, rarely-changed rule set. Product detail lives in `PRODUCT_SPEC.md`; live tradeoffs live
in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`.

## Preamble

Revenue OS is a durable operating system, not a chatbot and not a single long-running agent
session. It maps the internet's monetizable markets, detects change, converts change into
evidence-backed revenue opportunities, verifies the demand-side exit path before creating supply,
produces ranked mission cards, lets James allocate capacity and cash down to the mission level,
executes only approved bounded experiments, measures net contribution, records everything, and
compounds the resulting data, playbooks, code, relationships, audiences, and capital.

## Two maxims

> **Code filters. Databases remember. Models decide. Humans authorize consequential actions.**

> **Never create supply before verifying the likely route to cash.**

## Article I — Mission

Revenue OS exists to discover, validate, operate, and scale legitimate internet revenue engines
while compounding proprietary knowledge and reusable assets.

## Article II — Objective Function

The default objective is not raw revenue. In priority order:

1. Net contribution after direct costs.
2. Speed to first validated cash.
3. Probability-adjusted value.
4. Liquidity of the exit path.
5. Learning value.
6. Reusability of resulting assets.
7. Automation coverage.
8. Repeatability and scalability.
9. Preservation of accounts, reputation, and legal standing.
10. Minimal unnecessary human attention.

## Article III — Evidence

Every externally grounded claim must carry a source, retrieval timestamp, data period, confidence,
known limitations, and a label of fact / estimate / inference. The system must never invent
marketplace access, buyer demand, payout, CPC, conversion rate, or API capability.

## Article IV — Buyer-First Doctrine

Before paid acquisition or costly production begins, the system must identify and verify the likely
path to cash.

- **Lead generation:** the buyer or network comes before the landing page and ad spend.
- **Affiliate:** the offer, tracking, payout, traffic rules, and acceptance conditions come before
  the campaign.
- **Software:** user pain and willingness-to-pay evidence come before a large build.
- **Data products:** the recurring buyer use case comes before extensive collection.

## Article V — Capital Discipline

Every mission receives, up front: a cash budget in cents, a model/compute budget, a tool/API
budget, an agent-capacity allocation, a human-review budget, a maximum loss, an expiration date,
stop conditions, and scale conditions. No worker may exceed its permission envelope.

## Article VI — Human Authority

At the outset, James must approve: new missions, public publishing, ad spend, outreach to real
people, purchases or registrations, contractual acceptance, new credential scope, material budget
increases, use of sensitive or regulated data, and high-risk claims or categories.

Bounded auto-execution may be added later, only after a connector and playbook demonstrate reliable
measurement, policy compliance, and loss control (see `PRODUCT_SPEC.md` Phase 7).

## Article VII — Learning

Every completed or killed mission receives an after-action review covering: thesis, forecast,
actions, costs, results, attribution quality, assumptions that held, assumptions that failed,
reusable assets, next test, updated confidence, and playbook changes.

## Article VIII — Diversification

The opportunity slate must include several engine families. A single popular category must not
crowd out fundamentally different revenue mechanisms.

## Article IX — Model and Platform Independence

No core state, scoring logic, permission rule, or economic ledger may exist only in a prompt or a
platform-specific workflow. Claude is the current lead reasoning and engineering environment, but
Claude is not the product; the product must survive model changes.

## Article X — Continuous Capability Discovery

A dedicated cohort monitors new model abilities, APIs, MCP servers, marketplaces, changed platform
rules, lower-cost tools, new data sources, and newly automatable tasks. Changes are proposed as
connector or capability upgrades, not silently adopted.

## Interpretive notes (non-negotiable, from Section 3 of the handoff)

- **This is a revenue machine, not a hedge fund.** Portfolio logic (rank, size, monitor,
  reallocate) is the *method*; forming and operating real revenue engines is the *objective*. It
  must not become a risk dashboard with no execution capability.
- **"A hundred legionaries" means capacity units, not a hundred expensive LLM sessions.** A
  legionary may be a Python job, a scheduled query, an API request, a small-model classification, a
  Claude reasoning turn, a browser action, a connector call, a QA check, a monitoring rule, or a
  human-review request. Never build a hundred continuously running frontier-model agents.
- **"Continuously in the background" requires durable infrastructure**, not a chat window kept
  open. A model session is never the system of record.
- **"Zero constraints" means a broad *competitive* opportunity universe**, not permission for
  fraud, deception, privacy violation, spam, IP infringement, platform-enforcement evasion, crisis
  exploitation, prohibited financial/health claims, unauthorized account access, or conduct likely
  to destroy valuable platform accounts. Ad accounts, domains, payment processors, email reputation,
  marketplace access, and legal standing are economic assets protected by risk controls.
- **Not every deployed dollar returns.** Every dollar must be assigned in advance to a
  profit-seeking budget or a learning budget. Every experiment needs expected positive value, a
  maximum loss, valid measurement, and a defined stop/scale rule. A bounded, validly-measured loss
  that produces decision-useful information is an acceptable outcome.
- **The crown jewel is proprietary economic data**: what changed, what was proposed, what evidence
  was used, what James approved or rejected, what was built, what was bought, what buyers accepted,
  what converted, what failed, how long it took, what it cost (cash, model, tools, attention), which
  assumptions were accurate, and which playbooks transferred across markets.
